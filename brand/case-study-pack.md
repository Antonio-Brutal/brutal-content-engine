# The Brutal Case Study Pack

**Scope.** This pack defines the format of a Brutal case study: page shape, section order, lengths, and title mechanics. The editorial style pack applies here unchanged and in full: the closed-world rule, the [MISSING] marker discipline, the quote and anonymization rules, the confound rules, the em dash ban, and the banned-word lists. When this pack and the style pack conflict, the style pack wins. This pack makes exactly one named amendment to the style pack: the H1, the dek, and the About Brutal box name Brutal; body prose still says "we" and never "Brutal" in third person. Every example noun and number in this pack is radioactive per style pack §11: it teaches the pattern and may never appear in output.

## 1. Titles

**Default formula:** `How [Client] [concrete outcome] with Brutal`.

- The outcome slot holds a recorded fact: a number, a system, or a named change. Pattern: "How [named client] cut [recorded process] from [recorded before] to [recorded after] with Brutal."
- A number appears in the title only if it appears in the facts brief with a source class. No rounding in either direction. A number the brief hedges ("up to") stays hedged in the title.
- Anonymized clients take a category noun: `How a [industry category] [outcome] with Brutal`. Anonymization follows the style pack: if the brief records no written permission, the client name appears nowhere on the page, including metadata, URLs, and image alt text.

**Variants**, each gated on a checkable brief condition:

- `[Client] [present-tense verb phrase outcome] with Brutal`: only when the brief marks the engagement ongoing and records no completed outcome.
- `[Client]'s [system noun], built with Brutal`: only when the brief records a deliverable and no outcome metric. Comma, never an em dash.
- If no title can be assembled from recorded facts, the title slot stays empty and the producer note carries `[MISSING: title outcome]`.

**Banned:** essay titles, delayed-decode titles, colon formulas, mission-level abstractions, gerund titles, superlatives, and any outcome clause not traceable to a brief field.

## 2. The protagonist rule

The client is the hero. Brutal is the enabling partner, present only where it acted.

- **Client: third person, named** (or category noun). Neutral third-person narration throughout. Never first-person client narration: we did not live inside the client's head, and reconstructed interior voice is fabrication under the closed-world rule.
- **Brutal: "we", only in The solution section and the About Brutal box.**
- Verbs assign agency from the brief. The client decided, adopted, ran, measured. We built, wired, tested, handed over. "We transformed the client" fails twice: hype verb, wrong hero.
- People appear only if the brief records them, doing only what the brief records them doing. No composite characters, no "the team behind the platform."

## 3. Page anatomy, in order

The spine is the customer-story arc, per style pack §10: stat strip, The challenge, The solution, The results, one generalization paragraph, About boxes. Recorded facts only, client-approved.

1. **H1** per Section 1.
2. **Dek**: one sentence naming client, industry, and the headline outcome, with zero adjectives. If the brief records no outcome, the dek names client, industry, and deliverable, and the producer note carries `[MISSING: headline outcome]`.
3. **Stat strip**: 2 to 4 recorded numbers, each with a plain lowercase label and its brief-assigned source class (client-recorded, internal telemetry, or linked external). Exactly one recorded number: no strip, state it in the dek. Zero: no strip, no substitute. The strip precedes all narrative.
4. **The challenge**: the client's situation before, assembled from nouns the brief records. If the brief records fewer than three concrete before-state facts, the section shrinks to what exists; generic texture is never written to fill it. The section closes with the thesis in one sentence.
5. **The solution**: what we built, mechanism first, per the style pack §5 toolkit: components named, data flow stated, each part's job in plain English. No benefit claim without its mechanism in the same sentence.
6. **The results**: every number restated with its confound per style pack §6: objections stated first, causal verb undersold ("hard to attribute to anything else," never "proves"). A number with no recorded confound context gets `[MISSING: confound window]` in the producer note and a narrowed claim in the body.
7. **Generalization**: one paragraph meeting all four §4 requirements (named industry, stated data-age, three artifact types, layer mapping). No vendor mention inside it.
8. **Quotes**: recorded quotes only, verbatim, attributed exactly as recorded (name, title, company). Maximum two per page, placed in The challenge or The results. If the brief holds none, the page has none: no pull quotes, no paraphrase inside quotation marks, no `[MISSING: quote]` marker, because a quote is never required. Anonymized clients get anonymized attribution or no quote.
9. **About [Client] box**: 2 to 3 sentences from recorded facts only (founding year, size, market). Missing fields are dropped and listed in the producer note; the box shrinks.
10. **About Brutal box**, fixed pattern: "Brutal is an AI engineering agency. We build systems like the one in this story for companies that [pain class from this brief]." If the brief records no pain class, the slot reads `[MISSING: pain class]` for the human editor. Nothing else in the box.
11. **Sidebar facts list**: industry, company size, engagement length, systems involved. Fields without recorded values are dropped, not marked.

One CTA, at the end, per the solutions-page rule: a single plain declarative link. No mid-page CTA boxes, no share buttons.

## 4. Lengths and register

Length follows style pack §1, keyed to brief fields, never judgment. Section budgets when the brief supports them: The challenge 150 to 250 words, The solution 300 to 500 (the longest section), The results 150 to 300, boxes as specified. A thin brief produces a short page; padding is banned.

Register: identical to the blog register. Every §6 count applies unchanged. Named people act only in actions the brief records; scene detail comes from the brief or does not appear. Zero hype adjectives, zero exclamation marks, zero announcement-speak.

## 5. Conventions banned on sight

- Two-decimal precision without a source class, "6x" framing, stats repeated in headings or share-bait pull quotes.
- Pull quotes excerpted from narration, tweet-this units, vendor-written first-person customer diaries.
- Vendor-as-hero narration and competitor-displacement arcs, unless the switch is a recorded fact stated neutrally.
- Closing pitch sections, demo bands, per-vertical sales headings.
- Metaphor-driven section headings. Headings here are plain.

## 6. GEO alignment

Per the GEO pack, the page carries at least one extractable passage: client name (or category noun), metric, and timeframe in one sentence, one instance in the dek and one in The results. Every element traces to the facts brief. A sentence that cannot be assembled from recorded facts is not written; the producer note carries `[MISSING: extractable outcome sentence]`.
